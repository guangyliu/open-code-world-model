# Results, with the script and the asset behind each one

Everything on this page was produced by a script in this repository from conditions this
repository rendered. Videos are in `docs/gallery/video/` and `docs/video/`; the hosted pages
are built from the same files by `docs/mkgallery.py` and `docs/mkweb.py`.

## 1. Six released examples, reproduced end to end

`python examples/repro.py <N>` (needs the released examples at `CWM_EXAMPLES`). For each example
we author the scenario in our proxy from the authors' own prompt, then generate video **twice**
with MiniMax-H3 + CWM LoRA: once from their released conditions, once from ours, holding the
anchor image, the caption and the seed fixed. The proxy is the only variable.

| example | chosen for | outcome | panel |
|---|---|---|---|
| ex18 | water (class 2 = 52.8%) | near-identical shot; **placement tracks the proxy** | [compare_ex18.png](compare_ex18.png) |
| ex28 | vehicle at close range (class 10 = 15.2%) | subject and cart both placed correctly | [compare_ex28.png](compare_ex28.png) |
| ex30 | plain walk, no props | comparable; our vegetation blobbier, horizon higher | [compare_ex30.png](compare_ex30.png) |
| ex31 | animal protagonist (a stag) | both produce the stag; ours sits higher in frame, as our proxy places it | [compare_ex31.png](compare_ex31.png) |
| ex34 | flight | **our proxy disagrees badly, see below** | [compare_ex34.png](compare_ex34.png) |
| ex36 | wide composition (road 46.9%, sky 26.0%) | near-identical | [compare_ex36.png](compare_ex36.png) |

Videos: `docs/video/rel_exN.mp4` (driven by their proxy), `docs/video/our_exN.mp4` (driven by
ours), `docs/video/duv_*_exN.mp4` (the two proxies themselves).
Page: [guangyiliu.com/open-code-world-model](https://guangyiliu.com/open-code-world-model/).

The ex18 panel is the clearest evidence that the proxy is doing work: the figure sits centre-left
in *their* proxy and centre-left in the video driven by it; right-of-centre in *ours* and
right-of-centre in the video driven by ours. Same anchor, same caption, same seed.

**The counter-example we kept.** In ex34 their proxy is a high oblique vista over terrain, a lake
and winding roads; ours is a ground-level corridor. They disagree completely, and the two videos
come out nearly identical. The anchor and the caption carried that shot, not the proxy. On some
shots the video model is robust enough that the proxy contributes little, and a set of six
hand-picked successes would misrepresent that.

## 2. Gallery: does the LoRA read the proxy?

`python examples/gallery.py <scene>` authors six scenes anchored on a released first frame for
its style; everything after frame 0 is ours: the geometry, the actions, the camera, and the
caption compiled from the same simulation. Frame 0 is composed to the anchor by measurement
(subject centroid, lens-to-subject distance, sky share), not by eye.

| scene | anchor | what happens | frame 0 vs anchor: subject x, lens→subject |
|---|---|---|---|
| boulevard | ex40 | walk; a shuttle halts beside him; board; drive off | 0.424 / 0.417, 2.97 / 2.96 m |
| causeway | ex19 | run; a wagon passes; the boom pulls back 3.75→6.5 m | 0.501 / 0.501, 3.80 / 3.79 m |
| stream | ex4 | run along the river, break right, the rig swings round | 0.436 / 0.436, 3.93 / 3.87 m |
| witchroad | ex30 | walk, stand two seconds while the camera orbits 40°, walk on | 0.367 / 0.363, 1.83 / 1.76 m |
| frostcourt | ex25 | run and leap the curb, twice | 0.446 / 0.447, 3.90 / 3.83 m |
| monastery | ex13 | run, stop at the drums while the camera pushes in to 2.2 m, run on | 0.574 / 0.575, 4.02 / 4.03 m |

Each scene is generated by MiniMax-H3 with the **same anchor, proxy video, caption and seed**
in two ways: H3's native Ref2VA reference mode (`portb/run_cp.py --no-lora`; the anchor picture
and the proxy video are still supplied as references) and with the CWM LoRA merged. The only
variable is whether the model was taught to read depth and semantics.

- **Both modes follow the caption.** In native reference mode the boulevard shuttle still
  arrives, halts, and he climbs in. H3's reference mode reads the anchor and the prose well.
- **Only the LoRA follows the proxy's camera and layout.** When the proxy's boom rides the
  shuttle away, the LoRA video's camera goes with it; the reference-mode camera stays put. When
  the proxy turns the runner away from the river, the LoRA video turns him and the river leaves
  the frame. During the witchroad orbit the LoRA video's fence and spire wheel round him;
  reference mode holds the opening view.
- **Where the proxy adds little, the two are close**: frostcourt and monastery, whose actions
  are mostly along the line of the road.

So the LoRA is what makes the proxy a control signal rather than a mood board.

Two long takes (boulevard, frostcourt) are generated the authors' way: four windows of 124
frames with 90 new frames each, Retake34 overlap pinned, one compiled caption per window
(`portb/run_cp.py --windows 4 --window-seed-step`, stitched by `portb/stitch.py`).

Page: [guangyiliu.com/open-code-world-model/gallery](https://guangyiliu.com/open-code-world-model/gallery/).
Files: `docs/gallery/video/<scene>_{proxy,nolora,lora,lynn}.mp4`, `<scene>_4w_*.mp4`.

## 3. A second video model, zero-shot: LynnReal-Omni

[LynnReal-Omni](https://github.com/LynnReal-AI/LynnReal-Omni) is a 32B H3-architecture model
with native frame-aligned controls. `examples/lynn_inputs.py` turns a gallery scene into its
inputs: a flat-shaded control video rendered from our conditions (class colour shaded by depth,
solid vehicles via `CWM_SOLID_VEHICLES=1`) and our caption rewritten into its six-section
prompt. No LoRA, same anchor.

On all six scenes it follows the proxy's camera and layout at the level of H3 + CWM LoRA
(124 frames at 1344×768: 133 s of DiT on one H200). Wireframe vehicles do not survive it: the
model paints the yellow box edges as a real metal frame, which is why the solid-vehicle encoding
exists.

**Streamed chunk by chunk.** `experiments/lynnreal/chunk_drive.py` drives it 22 frames at a
time at 540p, each chunk a video-edit conditioned on the anchor and that chunk's proxy, with the
last frame of the previous chunk pinned into the first latent slot. The release has no entry that
takes a frame-aligned control and streams; this borrows its anchored structure-edit recipe.

| configuration (one H200, 21 new frames = 0.88 s per chunk) | conditioning | generate | DiT, 4 steps | × real time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| as released: cuDNN, every module staged to CPU between phases | 42 s | 37 s | 14.6 s | 90× |
| FlashAttention-3, nothing staged, text encoder pinned on the GPU | 2.5 s | 18 s | 13.4 s | 23× |
| same, Qwen vision blocks dropped from the text context | 2.3 s | 12.6 s | 8.0 s | 17× |
| no chunking: 124 frames in one call | 5 s | 65 s | 49 s | 14× |

What drifts is texture: the road coarsens chunk by chunk because each chunk restarts from one
frame (`experiments/lynnreal/drift.py` measures it). Carrying the release's bounded latent
history softens it; longer chunks nearly remove it; one 124-frame call is both the cleanest and,
per frame, the fastest. Which is the point: a bidirectional model wants the whole window.
For a model trained to stream, see
[streaming-code-world-model](https://github.com/TingtingLiao/streaming-code-world-model).

## 4. Physics and unit clips

`python examples/moves.py` renders 29 single-control clips (walk in each direction, drive in
six, camera translate and rotate on each axis, jump, board-and-drive, and the collision,
knockdown and run-over cases before and after the rules) with the applied control read back from
the rendered camera pose and subject position, so a clip whose keys disagree with its title is a
rig bug. `python test_actions.py` runs the 29 scenario tests.
